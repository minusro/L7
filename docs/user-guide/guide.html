L7

A 7-track looper for the Dirtywave M8. Quick user's guide.

L7 replaces the M8's own firmware with a live looper. You record loops from the M8's line input onto 7 tracks, shape each one (range, filter, volume, balance), and store combinations as scenes. You keep up to 12 projects on the SD card, and you can export any of them as an Ableton Live set.

The M8's keys are named on the device: the arrows, SHIFT, START, B (OPT), A (EDIT) and the power key. This guide uses those names.

The screen

Track rows: tracks 1 (top) to 7. Each row has three cells and a button.

Waveform: the recorded loop; the bright part is the range that plays, the line is the playhead.

Filter: the response of the track's highpass and lowpass filter.

Volume: the track's level and balance, with a level meter. Waveforms are drawn as if normalised: every clip's loudest point reaches the full height.

Button (right): the track number, filled while the track plays, outlined while it's muted, dim while it's empty.

Status row (bottom): the scene (SCN 3/7), the clock (bar.beat.step), the tempo, and the action button, which always shows what A will do.

The selected cell has a light frame. Move it with the arrows. The colour accents follow the loaded project's colour.

Recording

  1. Plug your source into the M8's line input. The input is always heard.
  2. Select an empty track and press A; the recording is set going when you let go of it. If the clock is stopped, it starts and recording begins on bar 1. If it's running, recording starts on the next bar line. If you press within about a quarter of a second after a bar line, it starts on that bar line instead (counted from when you pressed, as long as you let go soon after).
  3. Press A again to stop. Loops are always whole bars. If you stop within a beat after a bar line, the loop ends there; otherwise recording carries on to the end of that bar. The longest loop is 32 bars.
  4. The loop starts playing as soon as it's done, with no gap.

Pressing A on another empty track while one records stops the first and queues the next one to start exactly where the first ends. In a new, empty project a metronome click plays until the first loop is complete.

Changing the tempo re-pitches the loops, like a turntable. The tempo belongs to the project. You can change it while recording too: the new loop plays at the average tempo of what was recorded, in step with the clock over the whole loop. Exported to Ableton Live, it keeps a warp marker at every bar, so Live plays each bar as it was played.

The status row with A

  • Scene cell: go to the next scene when you let go of A (while playing, on the next bar line). After the last scene it goes round to scene 1. SHIFT + A duplicates the scene (the same as SHIFT + B twice).
  • Clock cell: play / stop.
  • Tempo cell: tap tempo. From the 3rd tap on, the tempo follows your taps, and keeps getting more accurate the longer you keep tapping (pause for over 2 seconds or press another key to start over); with the clock stopped, the 5th tap starts it from 1.1.1 (while it plays, tapping only sets the tempo). B + A sets the metronome: 0 off, A auto (only in a scene with no clips at all, muted ones included) or 1 on; each press changes whether you hear it, and the button shows a metronome, crossed out while it would click (with the clock running). SHIFT + A turns audio follow on or off (below); holding SHIFT there, the button shows a jack plug (the input), crossed out while audio follow is on.

Scenes

A scene is a snapshot of all 7 tracks: which clip each plays, its range, mute, filter, volume and balance. Whatever you change updates the current scene as you go; there is no save step.

  • SHIFT + UP/DOWN steps through the scenes. While playing, the change happens on the next bar line after you let go of SHIFT; the scene number flashes until then.
  • Below the first scene is 0*, an empty scene. Above the last is N+1*, a copy of the last one. Changing either turns it into a real scene. Tap UP or DOWN twice with SHIFT, holding the second tap, to jump to N+1* or 0*.
  • SHIFT + B twice duplicates the scene.
  • Cut a scene with SHIFT, then B, then A (in that order). Paste it after the current one with B, then SHIFT, then A. Cutting a scene with no clips removes it but leaves the clipboard as it was.

Following a MIDI clock

Connect a MIDI clock source (a drum machine, a DAW through a MIDI interface) to the M8's MIDI in, or send clock to it over USB (it shows up as a MIDI device on your computer). While it plays (from its Start until its Stop), L7 follows it: the tempo cell shows its tempo, and you can't change the tempo by hand meanwhile. While it's stopped, the tempo is yours to change, even if it keeps sending clock. Its Start starts L7 from 1.1.1 (or restarts it), with the bars lined up with the source's; its Stop stops it. If it continues from somewhere in the song (Song Position, then Continue), or jumps while playing, L7 starts there too, on the source's next beat, the clock showing that bar and every loop playing from where it would be. L7 keeps its beat in step by running a touch faster or slower for a beat when it drifts. Following a clock over USB, it plays 2 ms ahead of it, so what it sends back over USB audio lands on your DAW's beat. If you start L7 with START instead, the beats line up but not the bars. When the clock stops (or stops coming in), L7 keeps the average tempo it followed, ready for the next start, whether that's from the clock or from START. When the clock comes back at a clearly different tempo, L7 takes that one at once. L7 also sends its clock, Start and Stop, on the MIDI out and over USB, so other gear (or your DAW) can follow it. While a scene or project change waits for its bar line, the metronome stays silent.

Audio follow

L7 can follow the tempo and beat of whatever plays into its input (the line input, or audio from your computer over USB). Select the tempo cell and press SHIFT + A to turn audio follow on (the button shows a jack plug (the input) while you hold SHIFT; crossed out while it's on, and SHIFT + A turns it off again). It's remembered when you switch off. After about 5 seconds of music L7 finds the tempo, then the beat: the tempo cell shows the tempo it hears and its number flashes on each beat: in black (blinking off) while it's still looking for the beat (before it has a tempo, at the tempo shown), and in the accent colour once it has locked onto it. When the music stops, L7 keeps the tempo it had (the number flashes black again); when it comes back, L7 finds the beat again after a few beats. Press START on a beat and L7 starts exactly on it; while it plays, it keeps its beat on the music's, following tempo changes too. Only the beats are matched: bar 1 is where you pressed START. While audio follow is on you can't set the tempo by hand, but if it picks half or double the tempo you want, hold B on the tempo cell and press UP to double it, DOWN to halve it (LEFT/RIGHT move L7 earlier / later against the music, 10 ms a press): the clock stays on the music's beat (halved, on every other one). You can do this before it has found the tempo too: it then looks for the tempo near the one shown. Turning audio follow on again starts from the tempo it hears. A MIDI clock being followed takes priority.

USB audio

Connected to a computer, the M8 also shows up as an audio device called L7, with 16 channels to the computer and 2 from it. Channels 1 and 2 (Master) carry what you hear on the M8, master volume included, but without the metronome, so you can record L7 straight into your DAW. Channels 3 to 16 carry each track on its own, from the top row down: Ch 1 (the top row) on 3 and 4, Ch 2 on 5 and 6, and so on to Ch 7 (the bottom row) on 15 and 16. Each is the track as you hear it in the mix (its volume, balance, filter and mute) at full level whatever the master volume. Your DAW shows them by name ("Master L", "Ch 1 L" …), so you can record every track to its own channel at once. On Windows, if the PC has seen an older L7, remove it once in Device Manager (show hidden devices) so the new channels show up. While the computer records from L7, the M8's speaker is off (headphones still play). Audio the computer plays to L7 is mixed with the line input: you can monitor it and record it into loops like anything on the line input. While audio comes in over USB, the line input is left out of the mix after a couple of seconds of silence (so an empty input adds no noise), and comes back the moment something plays into it.

Audio from the computer arrives a little late (its audio buffer, its sync settings), so L7 measures by how much: play your DAW's metronome (or anything with a clear hit on each beat) into L7 for a few bars while the clock runs, and from then on loops you record from USB land on the beat. Change the DAW's buffer size and let it measure again the same way.

Resampling

Select an empty track and press B + A. L7 records everything you hear (tracks and input, but not the master volume) from the next bar line, like a normal recording. When it's saved, it moves to a new scene right after the current one, on the same track, and the clips that were muted come along, still muted. The action button shows a record dot with a plus while resampling is available.

Projects and the menu

Open the menu by pressing any arrow past the edge of the screen (LEFT or RIGHT past a row's ends, UP on track 1, DOWN on the status row; the a thick line along that edge of the screen shows while you hold the key). While the clock runs, the loaded project's card shows a small play icon. The clock keeps running. Go back with RIGHT or LEFT off the edge of the cards or on a list item, UP on the top row of cards, or DOWN on the last list item. Going back happens as soon as you press the arrow. Each screen keeps its own selection. Going off the top or bottom edge, you land on the other screen's bottom or top row, in the column you last used there; going off the left or right edge, you land on the other screen's far side, in the row you last used there. Keep holding the arrow and the switch happens after a moment (0.6 s) without letting go; the selection stays where it lands until you press again. With no project loaded, going back makes a new project in the first empty slot.

Cards A–L: the 12 project slots, A–D on the top row. A filled card is the loaded project, an outlined one has a project, a grey one is empty. L7 starts in the menu, on the loaded project's card (or the first empty card when none is loaded), with the clock stopped.

Exporting to Ableton Live

Load the project, open the menu and choose EXPORT ABLETON PROJECT. The clock stops and L7 writes a Live 11 project to the card, in /L7/EXPORTS/L7 A001 Project/ (the project's letter, then the next free number). The label then reads SAVED AS L7 A001, and the button stays off until you close the menu.

  • 7 audio tracks, coloured like the project, each with a highpass and a lowpass Auto Filter and a Utility.
  • One scene per L7 scene that has clips, plus an empty one at the end. The current scene's clips are armed to play when the set opens.
  • Each clip keeps its loop range, start, mute, volume (as clip gain), balance and filter settings (as clip envelopes).
  • The samples are copied into the project, so you can move it anywhere.

Storage mode: the card on your computer

In the menu, choose ENTER STORAGE MODE, then connect the M8 to your computer with a USB cable. The SD card mounts as a normal drive. The clock stops, and the screen shows the connection state.

When you're done, eject the drive on the computer, or press any key on the M8. L7 reads the card again and you're back in the menu.

Always eject or press a key before unplugging. Files you add, rename or delete on the computer are picked up when you leave storage mode.

Good to know

  • L7 saves as you go: tracks, scenes, tempo and the loaded project survive switching off.
  • When an action can't run right now (for example paste with an empty clipboard), the action button turns grey and A does nothing.
  • The headphone output mutes the speaker automatically.
  • MIDI clock and start/stop go out on the M8's MIDI out.
